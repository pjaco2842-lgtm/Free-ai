## 2. Why "75% win rate" is the wrong target

A trade's win rate is not a property of a strategy; it is a property of where you put the
target. For a win of $W and a loss of $L, break-even is `W / (W + L)`:

| target | win needed to break even | what one loss costs |
|---|---:|---:|
| 0.2R | **84.5%** | 5 wins |
| 0.3R | **78.0%** | 3.3 wins |
| 0.5R | **67.6%** | 2 wins |
| 1.0R | **50.7%** | 1 win |
| 2.0R | **33.8%** | half a win |

(One MES micro, ES ATR 70 pts, $350 risk at 1.0 ATR, $4.98 round-trip. Generated by
`trading/feasibility.py` → `trading/desk/feasibility.md`.)

So a 75% win rate is trivially producible: move the target closer than 0.5R and you get 80%,
85%, 90%. This project has already printed that result — `edge.py` found **92.9% win rate at
+0.21R**, a quarter-stop target that wins nine times in ten and **earns nothing**. That is
the single most dangerous number in the record. If a rule claims a 75%+ win rate, the first
question is not "does it hold up" but "what is its R, and what is its expectancy".

**The only metric that matters is expectancy per session:**

```
EV/trade   = p·W − (1−p)·L − costs
EV/session = EV/trade × trades per session
```

Worked examples at 1.0 ATR risk ($350 loss, $175 win at 0.5R, costs ≈ $5):

| rule | win | trades/session | EV/trade | **EV/session** |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| ES prior-low dip (current book) | 78% | 0.21 | +$44 | **+$9** |
| same rule at 2 micros | 78% | 0.21 | +$88 | **+$18** |
| hypothetical daily-firing 70% rule | 70% | 1.0 | +$18 | **+$18** |
| hypothetical 85% rule at 0.2R | 85% | 1.0 | **−$27** | **−$27** |

The 70% rule that fires daily beats the 78% rule that fires weekly, and the 85% rule loses
money. That is the whole economics of this problem, and it is why "get above 75%" is the
wrong instruction to hand an agent.

The second constraint is size. **$417 a session** (the three-day pace) at 0.5R needs about
**8 micros = $2,800 of risk against a $1,000 buffer**. There is no sizing that produces
$417/day on a 25K without the buffer being what pays for it:

| $/session wanted | micros at 0.5R | risk if all stop | share of buffer |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| $200 | 4 | $1,400 | 140% |
| $417 | 8 | $2,800 | 280% |
| $500 | 9 | $3,150 | 315% |

## 3. The four bugs that made everything look good

These are not theoretical. Each one produced a number that was believed, published, and later
retracted. **Check any candidate rule for all four before reporting it.**

### 3.1 Target that traded before the entry (the big one)

The book buys a limit at the prior day's low. Daily bars cannot order the high and the low.
The old engine asked "did the high reach my target?" and counted a win even when the high
came *before* the dip that filled the order. That is not look-ahead in the usual sense — it
is worse, because it is invisible in the output.

Fix, implemented in `trading/edge_causal.py`: a limit entry fills only if `low <= level`, and
its outcome is scored on the **close** (or a later bar) — never on the same bar's high. A
buy stop *above* the market may keep stop-first ties, because it is a legal order. The
`--selftest` flag demonstrates all three cases.

This is why the headline 77.7% / 80.4% win rate, 91% pass, 84% consistent pass, 14-session
median and the +$538 replay are **all retracted as evidence** (entry (o)).

### 3.2 Misaligned out-of-sample window

`aggressive.sweep_market()` scored the test half as `run(bars[cut:], S)` while `S` — the
indicator series — was built on the whole thing. `run` indexes `S[i]` and `bars[i]` together,
so every indicator the "test" half read belonged to the training half, offset by `cut` bars.

Truth: GC breakout 1.0/3.0/48 showed **+$590** in the harness test column; correctly split it
is **−$2,000**. CL: −$1,490 → −$1,166. (`trading/desk/retest-intraday.md`.)

### 3.3 Both orders filling

A rule that posts a long stop and a short stop on the same close, and then counts whichever
one the day rewarded, produced a **93% win rate** on `prevclose_break`. The bar cannot say
which filled first, so the honest treatment is to **skip the session** unless the open
already determines the side. That artefact is the origin of the "Oracle" — now a settled
dead end: 47–48% win, −$7 to −$16 per trade.

### 3.4 Selection among 287,280 configurations

`mine26k.py` swept 57,456 per market × 5 markets. **9,629** were positive in both halves;
**289** cleared every gate, against roughly **30 expected by chance** on a 95th-percentile
luck gate alone. Every survivor was `nday_fade`. Passing five gates after that many tries is
a reason to test forward, not a finding.

The correct habit: report `survivors / total tried` and the chance-expected survivor count
next to every result. A rule found by searching 57,456 variants is not "a 78% rule"; it is
"the best of 57,456, with an unknown amount of that being luck".

### 3.5 Independent draws per contract, and a cadence assumed to be daily

Found in this repo's own account walk on 2026-10-31, not in an old script. `sim()` drew a
separate win/loss for every contract in the position. Ten micros of one signal are **one
bet**, and they stop together, so this invented diversification and made oversized books
look safer than they are. Correct model: one outcome per session, multiplied by contracts.

Second half of the same bug: the walk traded **every session**, while the ES book fires
about **0.21 times a session**. A 60-session horizon at that cadence is only ~12 trades, so
most paths were still unfinished and the pass rate was badly understated.

Effect of the fix at a 78% win rate, 0.5R: two micros goes from an apparent **82% pass** to
**65%** (breach 18% → 35%), and one micro at the book's real cadence is **76% pass, 23%
breach, median 69 sessions**. Anyone quoting a pass rate must state: contracts share one
outcome, cadence, horizon, and the unfinished share — otherwise the number is not
comparable.

## 4. What is actually known, with sources

### Measured with correct ordering — and negative

`trading/desk/audit-search.md`: 6,000 open-entry configurations per market, 30,000 total,
one frozen rule per market chosen on 2009–2019 and then scored on later data.

| market | frozen rule | train $/trade | 2020–24 $/trade | p (random side) |
|---|---|---:|---:|---:|
| ES | prior_return_2 < −1, long, 1.5 ATR, 1R | +$13.92 | **−$0.50** | 0.475 |
| NQ | prior_return_2 < −1, long, 1 ATR, 2R | +$15.68 | +$21.35 | 0.202 |
| GC | prior_body_vs_prev > 0.5, short, 0.25 ATR, close | +$14.07 | **−$4.39** | 0.611 |
| CL | prior_body_atr < −0.5, long, 1.5 ATR, 2R | +$12.00 | **−$6.24** | 0.645 |
| NKD | prior_upper_wick > 0.5, long, 1.5 ATR, close | +$43.97 | +$79.91 | 0.226 |

**Not one clears p < 0.05.** These are the only rules in the repo ever tested with correct
entry ordering, and none of them is an edge.

### Measured — intraday, and negative

- `trading/desk/retest-intraday.md`: 15-minute breakout on GC = **−$16.35/trade**, random
  entries with identical stop/target/frequency/costs = −$3.62/trade, **p = 0.68**. CL:
  −$6.64/trade, **p = 0.79**. Commissions and slippage were **27%** (GC) and **68%** (CL) of
  the entire loss.
- 48-config walk-forward per market: GC 6 of 48 positive out of sample; CL 1 of 48.
- `trading/desk/scalp.md`: 15 market/timeframe cells, second half — portfolio **−$20,133 to
  −$23,838 over 59 sessions** (2,216 trades). ES 1-minute: 29.2 trades/day, 40% win, −0.49R.

So "trade more often" is not an untried idea. It is the idea that was tested and lost to costs.

## 7. Conventions that are not optional

From `HANDOFF-DESK.md` §5, and they exist because each was violated once:

1. **Every idea and its parameters go into `trading/RESEARCH-LOG.md` before it is believed.**
   Append new entries at the bottom, lettered in sequence, never edit or delete old ones.
2. Stops are sized from **yesterday's** ATR, never the bar's own.
3. When both stop and target are touched, the **stop wins**.
4. One tick of slippage per side plus $1.24 commission per side, always.
5. One position per market per session; skip ambiguous both-ways days.
6. **Paper only.** No live broker without a separate explicit request. Never read `.env`,
   never write a key anywhere, `.env` stays git-ignored.
7. Report `survivors / tried` and the chance-expected count beside every sweep result.
8. Do not present a replay as earnings, and do not quote a retracted number as current.
