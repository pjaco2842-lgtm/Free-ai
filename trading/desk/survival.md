## What the correction changed

The first version of this script drew an independent win/loss for every contract. That is not how a position works: ten micros on one signal stop together. The effect was large and it went the wrong way — it made size look safe.

- 78% win, 1 micro: **77%** pass (23% breach), median 14 sessions at a daily cadence.
- 78% win, 2 micros: **65%** pass (35% breach), median 4 sessions. The old model said 82% — that number was an artefact and is retracted.
- 78% win, at 0.21 trades/session: 1 micro **76%** pass (23% breach), median 69 sessions; 2 micros **65%** pass (35% breach), median 24 sessions.

So on this rule **size does not buy survival, it spends it.** At a 78% win rate the 75% floor is met by one micro and not by two, because two micros turn one ordinary loss into 70% of the buffer and the 40% consistency rule caps how fast the profit can be booked. The size that satisfies a 75% survival preference is **one micro** — which is also the size that was already chosen, for different reasons.

## Many attempts: the arithmetic you are describing

Per attempt, a funded evaluation is worth `V` and an evaluation fee costs `C`. Trying `N` accounts:

```
expected funded  = N x p
EV per attempt   = p x V - C
break-even p*    = C / V
```

| fee as a share of what a pass is worth | pass chance needed |
|---|---:|
| 5% | 5% |
| 10% | 10% |
| 15% | 15% |
| 20% | 20% |
| 30% | 30% |
| 50% | 50% |

If the fee is a fifth of what a pass is worth to you, anything above 20% pass is positive EV. A 75% floor is therefore a comfort threshold, not an economic one — the economic one is `p > C/V`, and it is far lower. What many attempts does **not** fix is an edge that is negative per trade: five copies of a losing rule is five losing accounts, and the fee is paid every time.

## What this cannot tell you

The win rate, the fill resolution and the true cadence. Everything here is arithmetic conditional on those three, and the sweep that produced the 78% is the one retracted in `RESEARCH-LOG.md` (o). Use it to choose a size once the causal re-measurement exists — not as evidence that the rule works.
